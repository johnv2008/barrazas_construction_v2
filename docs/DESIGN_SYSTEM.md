# Barraza's Construction — Brand & Design System

**Status:** Draft for approval — no implementation until sign-off
**Owner:** Creative Direction / Brand / UX
**Scope:** Applies to every public page and every future page. The admin CMS interior remains functional/utility-styled (see §14) and is out of scope for the visual language described here, except where noted.

This document is the single source of truth. If a future page, component, or piece of copy disagrees with this document, the document wins until it is formally revised.

---

## 0. Creative Concept

**"Built with Care" is not a tagline — it is a design instruction.**

Every decision in this system should be traceable back to one idea: *a family trusted this company with the most valuable thing they own, and that trust was earned through visible, deliberate craft.* Not flash. Not noise. Care.

The reference point is not other contractor websites — none of them are good enough to learn from. The reference point is how Porsche presents an engine bay, how a good architecture monograph presents a house, how Aesop presents a bar of soap. Restraint signals confidence. A company that is *actually* good at its job does not need to shout.

---

## 1. Brand Personality

**Brand Voice**
Direct, warm, competent. Speaks like a respected general contractor who has been doing this for twenty years and doesn't need to oversell — a straight answer, given plainly, is more persuasive than enthusiasm. Never cutesy, never corporate-jargon, never "synergy."

**Tone**
- Confident, not boastful
- Warm, not folksy
- Precise, not clinical
- Reassuring, not salesy

**Emotional Positioning**
Barraza's Construction should feel like the contractor a well-off, discerning homeowner's most trusted friend would recommend — the one who "actually shows up when they say they will."

**Customer Feelings — what we want a visitor to feel, in order, as they scroll**
1. "This company looks like they know what they're doing." (competence, first 3 seconds)
2. "This isn't going to be a nightmare." (relief — remodeling has a bad reputation for chaos)
3. "They've done homes like mine." (relevance)
4. "I could see them in my house." (trust, imagination)
5. "I should just reach out — it costs nothing to ask." (low-friction action)

**Brand Keywords**
Deliberate · Grounded · Precise · Warm · Enduring · Honest · Architectural · Quietly confident

**Things the brand should NEVER feel like**
- A national franchise template ("A+ Rated!", stock handshake photos, starburst badges)
- HGTV-style hype ("TRANSFORM your home NOW!!")
- A discount / bargain contractor (coupons, "% OFF" banners, urgency countdown timers)
- A tech startup (gradients, glassmorphism, emoji, playful mascot illustrations)
- Cold or corporate (all-charcoal, no warmth, sterile grid of stock icons)
- Cluttered or salesy (pop-ups, chat bubbles fighting for attention, exit-intent modals)

## 2. Creative Direction

**Design Philosophy — "Quiet Confidence"**
The design should get out of the way of the work. Every project photo is the star; the interface is the frame, not the art. When in doubt, remove an element rather than add one. If a section isn't earning its space, it doesn't belong on the page.

**Inspiration (why these, specifically)**
- **Porsche.com** — how a functional, technical product is presented with restraint and generous negative space; nothing competes with the product photography.
- **Restoration Hardware** — how materials (stone, wood, metal) are allowed to be photographed plainly and still look expensive because of the framing and pacing, not decoration.
- **A well-produced architecture monograph** (e.g. Phaidon or Taschen house books) — full-bleed photography, minimal caption typography, generous margins, one idea per spread.
- **Airbnb Luxe** — how trust and hospitality are communicated through calm UI and human, unstaged (but well-composed) photography rather than stock perfection.

**Composition Style**
Asymmetric, editorial layouts over centered/symmetric "template" layouts. Real architecture is rarely perfectly centered — neither should the page be. Favor large single images over busy grids. Favor one strong photo over three mediocre ones cropped to fit a grid.

**Photography Usage**
Photography is the primary design material, not a supporting graphic. Full detail strategy in §8, but the direction-level rule is: *when the photography is strong, let it run full-bleed and uninterrupted; when it's average, crop tighter, pair it with confident typography, and give it more breathing room so the frame does some of the work the photo can't.*

**Whitespace**
Generous, intentional, and asymmetric. Whitespace in this system is not "empty" — it is the pause between statements. A section that feels sparse on a contractor template should feel *edited* here. Minimum section padding is defined in §6; do not compress it to fit more content — cut content instead.

**Color Usage**
Warm neutrals do 90% of the work (backgrounds, structure, body copy). Burgundy is a *precision instrument*, not a wash — it marks the single most important action or accent per view (one primary CTA, one accent line, one active nav state), never large background fields. Black/ink is reserved for maximum-contrast moments (dark sections, footer, hero overlays) — never as a default text color when a warm charcoal will do.

**Typography Usage**
Two voices: a confident, architectural sans for structure (headings, UI, labels) and a soft editorial serif used sparingly for a small number of emotive, human statements (a founder quote, a manifesto line, a testimonial pull-quote). The serif should feel like a hand-written margin note next to a blueprint — rare enough to carry weight.

**Motion**
Motion confirms, it never entertains. Reveals are subtle proof that content loaded with intention, not a show. Full spec in §9.

**Communicating Trust**
Trust is communicated through *specificity*, not badges: real license numbers, real years in business, real cities served, real (even if imperfect) project photography, plainly stated process steps, and a website that is simply well-made — competence in craftsmanship and competence in digital presence read as the same signal to a homeowner.

**Communicating Luxury**
Luxury here is not gold leaf — it is editing. Fewer, better photos. Fewer, better words. Generous margins. A restrained, confident color palette. Slowness (in pacing and motion) reads as expensive; speed and clutter read as cheap.

## 3. Moodboard (real-world references, not websites)

**Materials:** honed white oak flooring, matte black steel window frames, book-matched marble veining, limewash plaster walls, reclaimed brick, powder-coated aluminum, brushed brass hardware, unlacquered fixtures that patina over time.

**Architecture:** California craftsman updated with modern restraint; Bay Area hillside modern (large glazing, cantilevers, board-form concrete); quiet minimalism (Napa/Sonoma wine-country residential); nothing baroque, nothing "McMansion."

**Textures:** linen, raw plaster, honed (not polished) stone, brushed metal, natural fiber rugs, unfinished/raw wood grain, matte ceramic tile.

**Lighting:** golden-hour exterior light (the same warm register the current hero photo uses), soft diffused daylight through large windows for interiors, warm 2700K-3000K practical lighting at dusk shots, never harsh flash-lit real-estate-listing lighting.

**Photography reference (style, not source):** architectural/interiors photography in the vein of Dwell magazine or a high-end real estate listing shot by a professional (not a phone snapshot) — natural light, corrected verticals, shallow depth of field on detail shots, wide/deep depth of field on room shots.

**Interior Design:** warm minimalism — white oak + white plaster + black steel + one warm brass or leather accent per room. Uncluttered staging.

**Construction (process imagery):** framing and structural work photographed with the same care as the finished result — clean job sites, organized tools, visible craftsmanship mid-process (this is an underused trust signal most competitors skip entirely).

**Landscape:** Bay Area hillside and coastal-adjacent greenery, drought-tolerant landscaping, natural stone hardscape.

**Lifestyle:** understated — a family at a kitchen island, hands on a countertop, morning light — never posed/stock "high-five with a hard hat" imagery.

**Color references (from the real world, informing §4):** aged wine/oxblood leather (the brand burgundy), unfinished raw plaster and limewash (warm white/stone), wet slate and charcoal-stained wood (ink/graphite), brushed brass and unlacquered hardware (a possible future warm metallic accent — not currently in system, flagged for consideration only).

## 4. Color System

Barraza's existing brand color was sampled directly from the company logo (`#800020`) — that provenance matters and is preserved as the one non-negotiable brand constant. Everything else in this palette is built to support it.

### Primary — Burgundy (brand signature; use sparingly, as an instrument not a wash)

| Token | Hex | Usage |
|---|---|---|
| Burgundy 900 (Deep) | `#5C0017` | Hover/active state for burgundy fills; text-on-light for maximum emphasis moments |
| Burgundy 700 (Core) | `#800020` | THE brand color — primary CTA fill, active nav underline, accent lines, icon accents, focus rings, links on dark backgrounds |
| Burgundy 500 (Light) | `#B3183F` | Hover state for burgundy 700 elements; links/accents on dark backgrounds |
| Burgundy 100 (Tint) | `#F3E4E8` | Subtle backgrounds for badges, quote blocks, or highlighted stat callouts — never for large surfaces |

### Neutrals (warm-toned — never cool/blue-gray)

| Token | Hex | Usage |
|---|---|---|
| Ink Black | `#1C1A19` | Headings on light backgrounds when maximum contrast is needed; dark section backgrounds (footer, hero scrims) |
| Graphite | `#3D3937` | Default body text color, secondary dark surfaces |
| Stone | `#E8E3DD` | Section background alternation, card backgrounds, dividers, subtle strips (trust strip, tags) |
| Warm White | `#FAF7F3` | Primary page background — replaces pure white almost everywhere; warmer, calmer, more premium than `#FFFFFF` |
| Pure White | `#FFFFFF` | Text on dark backgrounds, cards floating on Stone, header background |

### Semantic (muted, never neon — must feel like they belong in this palette)

| Token | Hex | Usage |
|---|---|---|
| Success | `#2F6F4E` | Form success states, confirmation messaging |
| Warning | `#B5792A` | Non-blocking form warnings, informational callouts |
| Error | `#A32A2A` | Form validation errors, destructive admin actions — intentionally distinct from Burgundy 700 so users never confuse "this is broken" with "this is the brand" |

### Interaction states

| State | Rule |
|---|---|
| Hover (burgundy fill) | Step to Burgundy 900; never lighten a fill on hover, always deepen (deepening reads as "pressed/premium," lightening reads as "cheap button") |
| Hover (text link) | Burgundy 700 → underline appears/extends, color does not change |
| Focus (all interactive elements) | 2px solid Burgundy 700 outline, 2px offset, on light backgrounds; 2px solid Pure White outline on dark backgrounds. Never remove focus rings. |
| Disabled | Stone fill, Graphite text at 40% opacity, no hover/focus response, `cursor: not-allowed` |
| Selection (`::selection`) | Burgundy 700 background, Pure White text |

**Color ratio guidance for any given viewport:** roughly 65–70% warm white / stone, 20–25% ink/graphite (text, dark sections used deliberately), 8–10% burgundy. If burgundy is covering more than ~10% of a screen at any time, pull it back — it has stopped being an accent.

## 5. Typography System

**Two-family system, deliberately minimal for both design discipline and page-weight discipline (no build pipeline, shared hosting — every font file has a real performance cost).**

- **Display / Heading — "General Sans"** (self-hosted WOFF2, OFL-licensed): a confident, architectural grotesk for all headings, navigation, buttons, and labels. Weights used: 600 (Semibold, default heading weight), 700 (Bold, hero-scale only).
- **Body — "Inter"** (self-hosted WOFF2, OFL-licensed): exceptional readability at small sizes, wide language support, pairs cleanly with General Sans without fighting it. Weights used: 400 (body), 500 (emphasis/labels).
- **Editorial Accent — "Fraunces" italic** (self-hosted WOFF2, OFL-licensed), used *only* for: a manifesto/founder statement, testimonial pull-quotes, and the occasional single emotive line in a hero or section intro. This family should appear on any given page **at most 1–2 times**. If it's used more than that, it stops being special.

*(Rationale for font choice: all three are free, self-hostable, well-hinted variable fonts that render crisply without a CDN or build step — consistent with the "no npm/node build required in production" hosting constraint already established for this project.)*

### Type Scale

| Role | Desktop | Tablet | Mobile | Weight | Line-height | Letter-spacing |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Display (hero H1) | 64px | 48px | 36px | 700 | 1.05 | -0.02em |
| H1 | 48px | 40px | 32px | 600 | 1.1 | -0.01em |
| H2 | 36px | 32px | 26px | 600 | 1.15 | -0.01em |
| H3 | 28px | 24px | 22px | 600 | 1.2 | 0 |
| H4 | 22px | 20px | 19px | 600 | 1.25 | 0 |
| H5 (card titles) | 18px | 18px | 17px | 600 | 1.3 | 0 |
| Body Large (lead paragraphs) | 19px | 18px | 17px | 400 | 1.6 | 0 |
| Body (default) | 16px | 16px | 16px | 400 | 1.65 | 0 |
| Small / Caption | 14px | 14px | 14px | 400 | 1.5 | 0 |

| Button | 15px | 15px | 15px | 600 (General Sans) | 1 | 0.02em |

**Text width rules**
- Body copy max measure: **640–680px** (≈65–75 characters per line) regardless of container width. Never let paragraph text stretch full-width on desktop.
- Headline max measure: constrain hero/section H1–H2 to roughly **14 words or 2 lines** at desktop width; if a headline needs a third line, it's too long — rewrite it, don't just let it wrap.

**Rules for headings**
Sentence case, never ALL CAPS, never title-case. One idea per heading — no semicolons, no "and" stacking two thoughts. Headings describe a *benefit or fact*, not a category label ("A kitchen built around how you cook," not "Kitchen Services").

**Rules for paragraphs**
Plain, direct sentences. Avoid adjective stacking ("stunning, gorgeous, luxurious kitchen" — pick one, or better, describe a specific detail instead of an adjective). Max 3–4 sentences per paragraph block on marketing pages.

**Rules for labels/eyebrows**
Always uppercase, always paired with the accent-line motif (short horizontal rule), always Burgundy 500/700 on light backgrounds or Pure White on dark. Used to categorize the section above a heading — never used as a heading substitute.

**Rules for buttons**
Verb-first, specific, sentence case ("Schedule a Consultation," not "Submit" or "Click Here" or "SCHEDULE A CONSULTATION"). Never more than 4 words.

**Rules for captions**
Used under images sparingly — only when a photo needs context a viewer couldn't infer (a project location, a "before" label). Graphite color, Small size, never italic (italic is reserved for the editorial accent family).

## 6. Spacing System

**Base unit: 8px.** Every margin, padding, and gap in the system is a multiple of this unit (4px permitted only as a "half-step" for fine typographic adjustments like icon-to-label gaps).

| Token | Value | Typical use |
|---|---|---|
| space-3xs | 4px | icon-to-text gaps |
| space-2xs | 8px | tight internal component padding |
| space-xs | 12px | form field internal padding |
| space-sm | 16px | default internal component spacing |
| space-md | 24px | card padding, small gaps between related elements |
| space-lg | 32px | gaps between distinct components within a section |
| space-xl | 48px | gaps between major content blocks within a section |
| space-2xl | 96px | space between grid columns at generous layouts |
| space-3xl | 120px | — |
| space-section (desktop) | 128–160px | vertical padding between major page sections |
| space-section (mobile) | 64–80px | vertical padding between major page sections, mobile |

**Containers**
- `container` (default reading width): max 1280px
- `container--wide` (photography-forward sections): max 1440px
- `container--narrow` (forms, FAQ, legal text): max 720px
- Side gutters: 24px mobile, 40px tablet, 64px+ desktop (fluid, not fixed, above 1440px viewport)

**Grid**
12-column grid at desktop (≥1024px), gutter 32px. 6-column at tablet, gutter 24px. Single column at mobile (≤640px), gutter 16px (= side padding).

**Card spacing**
Card internal padding: 24px (space-md). Gap between cards in a grid: 32px desktop / 24px tablet / 16px mobile.

**Section spacing rule**
No two adjacent sections may share the exact same background token (Warm White must not follow Warm White) — this is what creates the alternating rhythm that makes a long page feel structured rather than monotonous. See §10.

**Maximum content width**
No text block should exceed 680px measure; no full layout should exceed 1440px before it starts adding margin rather than stretching content further — this keeps ultra-wide monitors from turning the page into an uncomfortable horizontal sprawl.

## 7. Component Library

Each entry below is a *design spec*, not code. Naming is illustrative; actual class names are a developer decision within these constraints.

**Buttons**
- *Primary:* Burgundy 700 fill, Pure White text, 8px radius, 44px min height (touch target), 24px horizontal padding. Hover: Burgundy 900 fill. One primary button visible per viewport section — never two competing primaries side by side.
- *Secondary:* Transparent fill, 1px border (Graphite on light / Pure White on dark), text matches border color. Hover: border and text shift to Burgundy 700.
- *Ghost/Text link:* No border/fill, Burgundy 700 text, underline on hover only.
- *Disabled:* see §4 disabled state. Never hide a disabled button — show it disabled with a reason (helper text) if the user needs to understand why.

**Navigation (header)**
Solid Warm White (not transparent-over-hero — a fixed transparent header that swaps on scroll is a "template" tell and was deliberately removed from this build already). Logo left, 5–6 text links center/right, one primary button far right ("Schedule a Consultation"), phone number visible on desktop for the "I need this fixed today" segment. Mobile: hamburger → full-height drawer, Warm White, large touch targets, CTA button repeated at the bottom of the drawer.

**Footer**
Ink Black or Graphite background, Pure White/Stone text. Four zones: brand + license/insurance credentials, sitemap links, services list, contact block (phone, service area, social). Legal row (© year, Privacy, Terms) as a thin final strip, Small text, muted.

**Cards — Project Card**
Image (consistent aspect ratio per §8) top, 8px radius, no drop shadow at rest (shadows read as "app UI," not "architecture" — use a 1px Stone border instead). Title (H5) + category/location (Small, Graphite) below. Hover: image scales 1.03 (subtle, 400ms), no card lift/shadow-pop.

**Cards — Service Card**
Same visual language as project cards for consistency, but content is icon/short-description/link rather than a single hero photo.

**Forms / Inputs**
Label above field (never placeholder-as-label — placeholders disappear and hurt usability/accessibility). 1px Stone border, 8px radius, 12px vertical / 16px horizontal padding, 16px body text. Focus: border becomes Burgundy 700, 2px, plus the standard focus ring. Error: border becomes Error red, helper text below in Error red, Small size. Required fields marked with a subtle asterisk, not color alone.

**Dropdowns / Selects**
Same frame as text inputs; native `<select>` styling overridden minimally (custom chevron icon only) — do not rebuild a fully custom dropdown component unless a specific UX need (e.g. searchable project-type list) justifies the added complexity.

**Upload Fields**
Dashed-border drop zone (Stone border, Warm White fill), centered icon + "Drag a photo here or click to upload" copy, file preview thumbnail once selected with a remove (×) affordance. Matches the existing single optional-photo upload already in the consultation form.

**Checkboxes / Radio Buttons**
Custom square (checkbox) / circle (radio) at 20×20px, 1px Graphite border at rest, Burgundy 700 fill + Pure White check/dot when selected. Label always clickable (large hit target), never rely on the input alone.

**Accordion (FAQ)**
Single column, max 720px width. Question row: H5 weight, chevron icon right, 1px Stone bottom border. Click reveals answer (Body size, Graphite) with a simple height/opacity transition (see §9). Only one open at a time on mobile is *not* required — allow multiple open simultaneously; don't force homeowners to re-find a question they already opened.

**Tabs**
Used sparingly (e.g. switching between "Included / Not Included" on a service page). Underline-style, not boxed/pill — an underline tab under the burgundy accent-line motif reads as more architectural than a pill/segmented control.

**Pagination**
Used on the Projects gallery if it grows beyond ~12–16 items. Numbered, not infinite scroll — infinite scroll undermines the "curated, edited" feeling this brand needs. Simple text-based prev/next + numbers, Burgundy 700 for active state.

**Breadcrumbs**
Small text, Graphite, `>` or `/` separator, used on Service sub-pages and individual Project pages only (not homepage/top-level pages). Last (current) item not a link, Ink Black.

**Gallery / Lightbox**
Masonry or fixed-aspect grid (see §8 for photography rules) → click opens a full-viewport lightbox, Ink Black backdrop at 92% opacity, image centered with generous margin, simple prev/next arrows, close (×) top-right, caption below if present. No zoom/pan gimmicks.

**CTA Blocks**
Full-width band, alternates between Stone and Ink Black backgrounds depending on position in page rhythm (§10). Centered content, max 640px, H2 + one sentence + one primary button. Used at natural decision points (after Services, after Featured Project, before Footer).

**Testimonials**
One at a time (carousel or static single-quote block), never a wall of 9 testimonial cards — that reads as desperate for proof rather than confident. Fraunces italic for the quote (this is one of its 1–2 sanctioned uses per page), attribution below in Small/Body: name, city, project type — never anonymous ("- Happy Customer").

**FAQ**
See Accordion above. Content strategy for *what* goes here is in §11.

**Statistics**
Used sparingly — 3–4 max, not a wall of counters. Large number in Display/H1 styling (Burgundy 700 or Ink Black), short label below (Small, uppercase, Graphite). No animated counting-up gimmick (violates the "nothing flashy" motion rule).

**Timeline (Process)**
Horizontal at desktop (already implemented as 5 compact steps), vertical stacked at mobile. Numbered (01–05), 2px top border in Line-on-light color, burgundy for the number itself. No connecting line/dot-and-line graphic — the numbers alone carry it, an actual connecting line reads as a "how it works" template cliché.

**Icons**
Custom, thin-stroke (1.5px) line icons that echo the linework of the company logo (roofline/tools). No filled/glyph icon sets, no emoji, no third-party icon-font "kitchen sink" packs mixed with custom icons — visual consistency of stroke weight matters more than icon variety.

**Empty States**
(Admin-facing primarily, e.g. "No leads yet.") Simple centered icon + one sentence + one action if applicable. Never a full illustration — illustrations don't match this brand's material/photographic identity.

**Loading States**
Skeleton blocks (Stone background, subtle shimmer) for image-heavy areas; a simple inline spinner (thin burgundy ring) for form submissions. No full-page spinners/overlays unless genuinely blocking (e.g. mid-form-submit).

**Error States**
Plain language, no stack traces or technical codes shown to visitors. "Something went wrong on our end — please try again or call us at [phone]." Always give a fallback path (phone number) since a broken form is exactly the moment a homeowner needs the reassurance that this company is still reachable.

## 8. Photography System

The client does not have professional photography. This is a design constraint to be solved deliberately, not a reason to fall back on stock imagery (an exception was made once, for the homepage hero, at explicit client request — that should remain the exception, not the pattern; see the flag raised when that photo was added).

**Cropping**
Standardize on three ratios, used consistently by context, never freeform:
- **3:2** — wide "hero/establishing" shots (exteriors, full-room shots)
- **4:3** — standard project/detail photography (most existing client photos)
- **1:1** — thumbnails, contact-sheet-style supporting grids

Never stretch or distort a source photo to force a ratio — crop to it. If a photo can't be cropped to any of these three without losing the subject, it isn't strong enough to use; find another or shoot it again.

**Framing**
Prefer tight, intentional crops that isolate a genuinely good detail (a marble seam, a cabinet pull, a light fixture) over a wide shot that shows a cluttered or unstaged room. When a wide "hero" shot is genuinely strong, let it run large and uncropped-feeling — don't shrink a great photo to make room for a mediocre one next to it.

**Pairing images**
Pair one "hero-worthy" wide/establishing shot with one or two tight detail shots from the *same* project and, ideally, the *same* lighting session — mismatched color temperature or time-of-day between paired images is the single fastest way to look amateur. Never pair two images that were shot years apart in obviously different lighting conditions in the same component.

**Before/After**
Present as a clearly labeled two-up (not a slider gimmick, which was deliberately removed from this build already for feeling gimmicky/dramatic) — "Before" and "After" as small caption labels, matched framing/angle as closely as the source photos allow. If the "before" shot's angle doesn't reasonably match the "after," don't force the pairing — use the after shot alone with a written description of what changed.

**Volume per section**
Fewer, better: 1 hero image, 1 featured project (1 main + up to 2 supporting), 4 service images, 4 selected-project thumbnails. That's the entire homepage photography budget. Resist the urge to add a gallery grid "because we have more photos" — a thin but excellent selection outperforms a deep but inconsistent one.

**Compensating for inconsistent quality**
- Consistent color grading pass on every photo before upload: warm white balance, slightly lifted shadows, restrained saturation (a single repeatable edit "recipe," not per-photo guessing).
- Consistent crop ratios (above) so inconsistent original framing doesn't show.
- Consistent corner radius (8px) and consistent border treatment (1px Stone) applied uniformly regardless of source quality — the *frame* provides consistency the *photos* individually can't.
- When a photo is genuinely weak (poor lighting, cluttered background, low resolution), use it small/thumbnail-only rather than hero-scale, or omit it — don't force every available photo onto the page.

**Overlays**
Only functional, never decorative: a gradient scrim behind hero text for legibility (as already implemented), nothing else. No duotone-everything, no texture overlays, no vignettes for "mood" — the photography should look like a well-shot photograph, not a filtered one.

**Consistency mechanism going forward**
As real client photography accumulates, establish a lightweight intake rule: every new project photo set gets the same edit recipe and crop-ratio pass before it enters the CMS media library — this belongs in the admin workflow (§14), not left to whoever is uploading that day.

## 9. Animation System

**Nothing flashy. Motion earns its place by confirming something happened — it never performs.**

| Property | Value |
|---|---|
| Micro-interaction duration (hover, focus) | 150ms |
| Standard transition duration (reveals, state changes) | 250–300ms |
| Large reveal duration (hero content stagger, page-section entrance) | 500–600ms |
| Easing, standard | `cubic-bezier(0.22, 1, 0.36, 1)` (confident ease-out, no bounce/overshoot) |
| Easing, hover/return | `ease-in-out` |
| Scroll-reveal distance | 12–16px translate-Y, fade from 0→1 opacity |
| Scroll-reveal stagger (grids) | 60–80ms between siblings |
| Reduced motion | All durations collapse to ~0ms; transforms removed; content appears in final state instantly. This is not optional — always implement via `prefers-reduced-motion`. |

**Hover behavior:** buttons deepen color (never scale/bounce); cards scale their *image* subtly (1.03×), never the whole card (a scaling card shadow-pop reads as a generic UI kit, not architecture); links underline-extend, don't change color abruptly.

**Image reveals:** simple fade+rise on scroll-into-view, once per element (don't re-trigger on scroll-up/down), respecting reduced motion.

**Scroll animations:** limited to the fade-rise reveal above. No parallax, no scroll-jacking, no pinned/sticky scroll sections — these were deliberately removed from an earlier design pass for feeling "too dramatic," and that judgment holds at the system level.

**Button interactions:** 1px translate on `:active` (press feedback) — nothing more.

**Navigation:** mobile drawer slides/fades in ~200ms; desktop nav has no animated show/hide (it's always present, solid).

**Page transitions:** none. This is a server-rendered multi-page PHP site by design — do not simulate SPA transitions; a plain, fast page load is more trustworthy than a transition effect trying to disguise it.

## 10. Layout System

Reusable patterns — every page should feel like it belongs to the same family by reusing this limited set rather than inventing new layouts per page.

- **Hero:** Full-bleed photograph, gradient scrim, bottom- or center-left anchored content block (as currently implemented on the homepage). Sub-pages (Services, individual Project) use a shorter variant — same pattern, reduced height, no full-viewport drama.
- **Split Section:** 50/50 (or 55/45) two-column, image one side / text the other, alternating sides page-to-page to avoid monotony (never alternate *within* a single page more than once or twice — too much alternation feels restless).
- **Editorial Layout:** Asymmetric single-column-emphasis layout for a manifesto/founder statement — generous margins, Fraunces pull-quote, minimal supporting imagery.
- **Gallery:** Fixed-ratio grid (§8 ratios), consistent gutter (space-md/lg), optional lightbox.
- **Project Story:** Long-form case study template — hero image, short meta line (category/city/scope), narrative body copy (max measure), 2–4 supporting images interspersed, closing CTA.
- **Service Layout:** Icon/short-copy card grid for the overview (Services hub), full editorial split-section template for each individual service sub-page (what it is / who it's for / process specifics / related projects / CTA).
- **FAQ:** Narrow single-column accordion, max 720px, centered.
- **CTA Band:** Full-width color block (Stone or Ink Black), centered content, one action.
- **Forms:** Two-column desktop (context/copy left, form right — as currently implemented for consultation), single column stacked on mobile.
- **Alternating Layouts (page rhythm rule):** Background tokens must alternate section-to-section (Warm White → Stone → Warm White → Ink Black → Warm White...). No identical background appears twice in a row. This single rule is what makes a long homepage or service page feel composed rather than like a stacked list of blocks.
- **Grid / Magazine Layout:** Used for the Projects hub — an editorial, unevenly-sized grid (a few "featured" large tiles among standard tiles) rather than a uniform Pinterest-style masonry, to preserve the "curated" feeling.

## 11. Content Strategy

**Headlines:** State a concrete benefit or fact tied to the reader's actual situation, not a category label. *"A kitchen built around how you actually cook"* beats *"Beautiful Kitchen Remodeling."* Every headline should fail the test of "could this sentence appear on a competitor's site unchanged?" — if yes, it's too generic.

**CTAs:** Verb-first and specific about what happens next ("Schedule a Consultation," "See This Project," "Call (XXX) XXX-XXXX") — never "Learn More," "Submit," or "Click Here."

**Service descriptions:** Three-part structure — *what it is* (plain description), *who it's for* (a homeowner situation they'll recognize: "if your kitchen still has the layout from 1994..."), *what's actually included* (concrete scope, not vague adjectives). Avoids the generic "we do X, Y, Z" list every competitor uses.

**Project descriptions:** Narrative arc — the situation/challenge the homeowner had, the approach taken, the result. Specific details (materials, timeframe, a real complication that was solved) build far more credibility than superlatives ("stunning," "gorgeous") ever do.

**FAQs:** Answer the actual objections a homeowner is silently holding — cost ranges (even approximate), permit/timeline realities, how disruptive the process is to daily life, how change orders are handled, license/insurance verification. Plain English, no hedging legalese. This is a trust-building surface, not a legal-liability surface — write it that way.

**Testimonials:** Presented one at a time with full attribution (name, city, project type). Prefer a testimonial that mentions a *specific* moment or detail over generic praise ("They caught a plumbing issue before it became a problem" beats "Great experience!").

**SEO blended into premium design:** Keywords belong in headings, image alt text, and page titles/meta descriptions — written naturally, as the honest and specific version of that content would already read (a well-written, specific headline is already good SEO). Never add a visible keyword-stuffed paragraph block at the bottom of a page "for SEO" — that is both bad for the brand and increasingly ignored by search engines anyway.

## 12. Conversion Strategy

**Lead flow:** One primary conversion path sitewide — the consultation form (already built, intentionally simplified to a single step). Phone number is the secondary, always-visible path for homeowners who want to talk immediately. Never present a third competing conversion mechanism (no chat widget, no separate quote calculator, no newsletter signup competing for attention) — a homeowner facing a $50k decision should have exactly one clear next step, not three.

**CTA placement:** Hero, immediately after Services (once they understand the offering), immediately after the Featured Project (once trust peaks), and the Consultation section itself before the Footer. That's the rhythm: *understand → trust → act*, repeated only as many times as natural pauses in the page occur — not on every scroll.

**Trust placement:** Trust signals sit *immediately adjacent to* friction points, not off in a separate "About" silo — license/insurance line directly by the form, years-in-business in the hero trust line, real project photography directly above the CTA that follows it.

**Contact strategy:** Two tiers by intent — form for "I'm planning, tell me more" (lower urgency, more detail given), phone for "I need to talk to someone now" (higher urgency). Both must be reachable within one scroll/tap from any page.

**Consultation strategy:** Position the first form submission as low-commitment and informational (matches the existing hidden-default framing already in the LeadController: "exploring" timeframe, "not sure" budget) — never ask for a firm budget or timeline upfront in the initial form; that's a second-conversation question, and asking too early increases abandonment.

**Psychology at play:**
- *Loss aversion:* remodeling has a reputation for going badly — the entire design language exists to counter the fear of "picking the wrong contractor," more than to sell excitement about the end result.
- *Social proof:* specific, attributed testimonials and real (not stock) project photography.
- *Authority:* license/bond/insurance stated plainly and specifically, years in business, a clearly explained process (uncertainty about process is a bigger deterrent than price).
- *Reciprocity:* a genuinely free, no-pressure consultation framed as such.
- *Reduced friction:* the shortest form that still qualifies a lead (already implemented — name/phone/email/city/type/description/optional photo), no account creation, no multi-step wizard.
- *Increased trust:* the website's own visual polish and speed are themselves a trust signal — a homeowner subconsciously reasons "if they cared enough to get their website right, they probably care enough to get my kitchen right."

## 14. Admin CMS Strategy

The public design system above is what content managers *produce*; the admin's job is to make producing it easy without requiring a developer for routine updates. This maps directly onto the architecture already in place (PDO/MariaDB, no ORM, existing `leads`/`lead_attachments` tables and admin auth system) — nothing here requires a new stack.

| Frontend surface | Admin-managed via |
|---|---|
| Projects (hub + individual pages) | `projects` module (new): title, category, city, scope summary, narrative body, status (draft/published), ordering/featured flag |
| Project images | `project_images` module (new), linked to a project: image, alt text, sort order, ratio tag (matches §8 crop ratios) |
| Testimonials | `testimonials` module (new): quote, name, city, project type, optional link to a project |
| Services | `services` module (new): title, short description, icon reference, hero image, full sub-page body copy |
| SEO | Per-page fields (already scaffolded in Phase 1 pattern via controller `title`/`metaDescription`): meta title, meta description, OG image — exposed as editable fields once pages become CMS-driven rather than hardcoded in controllers |
| Hero | `page_sections` (already referenced in `HomeController`'s data-shape comments as the eventual source): heading, lead copy, CTA labels/links, hero image reference |
| Gallery / media library | A shared image-upload module reused by Projects, Services, and Hero — enforces the crop-ratio and file-validation rules from §8 at upload time (this is where the color-grade/crop consistency workflow from §8 should live operationally) |
| Forms / Leads | Already built — `LeadController`, `leads`/`lead_attachments` tables, admin dashboard lead list. No change required; this document does not touch it. |

**Principle:** every piece of copy or image that a non-technical business owner would reasonably want to change themselves (a new project, an updated phone number, a new testimonial) belongs in the CMS. Structural layout and the design system itself do not — that stays code-level, governed by this document.

## 15. Design Rules

**Developers MUST**
- Treat `variables.css` (or its evolution) as the only source of color/type/spacing values — no hardcoded hex codes, pixel values, or one-off font-sizes in component/page CSS.
- Preserve the alternating section-background rhythm (§10) on every new page.
- Apply the standard focus-visible treatment to every interactive element, no exceptions.
- Implement `prefers-reduced-motion` for every animation added, no exceptions.
- Keep body copy measure ≤680px regardless of container width.
- Compress and standardize every uploaded photo's crop ratio per §8 before it ships to production.
- Reuse an existing component/layout pattern before inventing a new one.

**Developers MUST NOT**
- Introduce a new color, font family, or spacing value outside this system without a documented system update first.
- Use box-shadow "card lift" hover effects, gradients-as-decoration, or glassmorphism — none of these belong to this brand's material language.
- Add a slider, carousel-with-autoplay, parallax, or scroll-jacking effect without explicit creative-direction sign-off (these have already been tried and rejected once in this project for feeling dramatic/gimmicky).
- Stack more than one primary-styled CTA in the same viewport.
- Use stock photography as a default fallback — the one existing exception (homepage hero) was an explicit, flagged, one-off client decision, not a pattern to repeat.
- Touch backend/PHP/routing/auth/CMS logic while doing visual system work — this document is front-end/visual scope only.

**Designers MUST**
- Design every new page from the existing layout pattern library (§10) before proposing a net-new pattern.
- Justify any new component by showing it can't be achieved by composing existing components.
- Maintain the 65/25/10 warm-white/dark/burgundy balance guidance (§4) when proposing new sections.
- Design mobile and desktop together, not mobile "derived after the fact" from a desktop comp.

**Designers MUST NOT**
- Propose decorative elements that don't serve hierarchy or comprehension (decorative numbering, background grid patterns, unnecessary icons — these were deliberately stripped from an earlier pass of this exact site for feeling "too aggressive").
- Design a section that requires photography the client doesn't have and can't reasonably obtain before launch.
- Increase type scale or section padding beyond §5/§6 values "to make it feel bigger" — bigger was already tried and explicitly rejected by the client as "too dramatic."

## 16. Homepage Wireframe

*(Description only — informed by, and formalizing, the section order already validated on the live homepage. No new sections are being proposed here; this is the system-level rationale for the structure already in place, so future pages built from this system stay consistent with it.)*

1. **Hero** — *Purpose:* immediate credibility + orientation. *Content:* eyebrow (company name), H1 (concrete brand promise), lead sentence, two CTAs (primary: consultation, secondary: view work), 2-line trust strip. *Components:* full-bleed photo hero, gradient scrim, primary/secondary buttons. *Hierarchy:* H1 is the loudest element on the page; nothing on the homepage should out-weigh it. *Conversion role:* first CTA exposure.

2. **Trust Strip** — *Purpose:* immediate, low-effort credibility reinforcement right below the emotional hero beat. *Content:* 3 short facts (years in business, licensed/bonded/insured, specialization). *Components:* thin Stone band, dot-separated list. *Hierarchy:* deliberately quiet — Small text, no heading — this is a footnote to the hero, not a competing statement.

3. **Company Introduction** — *Purpose:* answer "who is this company" briefly before asking for any commitment. *Content:* short H2 + 2–3 sentence body + one supporting image. *Components:* Split Section. *Conversion role:* none directly — this section's job is trust, not action.

4. **Services** — *Purpose:* show breadth of capability, let the visitor self-identify their project type. *Content:* 4 service cards (Kitchen/Bath/Whole-Home/Additions-ADU), each linking onward. *Components:* Card grid, Service Card. *Conversion role:* first re-engagement CTA band directly follows this section per §12.

5. **Featured Project** — *Purpose:* proof, at the moment trust needs to peak before the ask. *Content:* one substantial project story (main image + 1 supporting image + short narrative + link). *Components:* Featured Project split layout. *Conversion role:* trust peak, sets up the next CTA moment.

6. **Selected Projects** — *Purpose:* breadth of proof after the depth of the featured project. *Content:* 4 project thumbnails. *Components:* Card grid, Project Card.

7. **Why Choose Barraza's** — *Purpose:* explicit, scannable trust reinforcement for a visitor who's now seriously considering reaching out. *Content:* 4 short benefit statements. *Components:* Benefits grid (no icons needed — text alone, understated).

8. **Process** — *Purpose:* remove the single biggest fear (chaos/uncertainty) by making the process concrete. *Content:* 5-step numbered timeline. *Components:* Timeline (horizontal desktop/vertical mobile).

9. **Planning Guidance** — *Purpose:* answer practical pre-contact questions (scope, budget, permits, timeline, materials) so the form that follows feels like a natural next step, not a cold ask. *Content:* 5 short guidance items. *Components:* Planning grid.

10. **Consultation (Form)** — *Purpose:* the primary conversion point. *Content:* short reassurance copy + the simplified single-step form. *Components:* Consultation split layout, Form component. *Conversion role:* this is the page's terminal goal — everything above exists to make this feel low-risk by the time a visitor reaches it.

11. **Footer** — *Purpose:* final trust/utility layer and secondary navigation for visitors not ready to convert. *Content:* brand + credentials, sitemap, services, contact block, legal row.

## 17. Developer Handoff

**How this system maps onto the existing PHP/CSS architecture (no rebuild required):**

1. **Tokens first.** Extend the existing `public/assets/css/variables.css` custom-property approach to formally encode every value in §4–§6 (color, type scale, spacing scale) as named custom properties, exactly as burgundy/warm-white/etc. already are today. This document's tables *are* the target values for that file — implementation is a direct transcription, not new design work.

2. **Components second.** Extend `public/assets/css/components.css` one component at a time from §7, reusing the existing pattern of shared classes consumed via `App\Core\View::component()` (as `service-card.php`, `project-card.php`, `project-form.php` already do). Do not introduce a second styling paradigm (e.g. a utility-class framework, CSS-in-JS) — this is a hand-authored CSS system by design, matching the no-build-step hosting constraint.

3. **Fonts.** Self-host General Sans, Inter, and Fraunces as subset WOFF2 files under `public/assets/fonts/`, loaded via `@font-face` in `base.css` with `font-display: swap`. No external font CDN calls (keeps the CSP simple and avoids a third-party dependency on shared hosting).

4. **Pages third.** New pages (§13 sitemap) are assembled from the layout patterns in §10 and components in §7 — a new page should almost never require new CSS beyond page-specific content wiring; if it does, that's a signal to check whether an existing pattern was skipped.

5. **Photography pipeline.** Formalize the crop-ratio/compression workflow from §8 as a documented step in the admin media-upload flow (§14) — even a simple documented checklist for whoever uploads project photos (crop ratio, color-grade pass, `sips`/equivalent compression before upload, matching the compression approach already used for the homepage hero) keeps consistency without requiring new tooling.

6. **Backend is unaffected.** Nothing in this document requires changes to `App\Core`, `App\Services`, `App\Middleware`, routing, authentication, or the CMS's functional logic. New CMS modules referenced in §14 (`projects`, `project_images`, `testimonials`, `services`) are additive schema/controller work for a future phase — scoped, planned, and explicitly *not* authorized by this document alone.

7. **Rollout order recommendation:** (a) tokens, (b) typography + fonts, (c) component-by-component pass on existing homepage (should mostly already conform — this document formalizes decisions largely already made during the recent reduction/refinement pass), (d) net-new pages in sitemap order of business priority (Services sub-pages and Projects hub are likely highest-value next pages), (e) CMS modules to make (c)/(d)'s content editable without a developer.

8. **Governance.** Any future request to deviate from a value in this document (a new color, a bigger hero, a slider) should be treated as a *system change request*, not a one-off page edit — update this document first, then implement, so the system and the live site never silently diverge.
